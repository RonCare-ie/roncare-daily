Today's Briefing

Family Carers Facing Acute Financial Pressure

A new Family Carers Ireland report reveals 71% of family carers struggle to make ends meet, with nearly half cutting back on essentials like food and heating.

Nearly half of all family carers in Ireland — 48% — have been diagnosed with a mental health condition. Not burned out. Not stressed. Diagnosed. Behind that number are hundreds of thousands of real people: mothers sitting at kitchen tables at midnight wondering how much longer they can keep going, fathers who haven't had a full night's sleep in years, adult children quietly falling apart while holding everyone else together. Caring is an act of love, but the toll it takes on the person doing it is something Ireland has been far too slow to talk about.

The figures are hard to sit with. Among Irish family carers, 35% have been diagnosed with depression and 39% with anxiety. The average carer provides 38.7 hours of care per week — a full working week, unpaid — and 29% are providing more than 43 hours. That kind of relentlessness doesn't leave much room to notice your own distress, let alone do something about it. Many carers don't reach out because they feel guilty putting themselves first, or because they simply don't have the time.

What makes this harder is the isolation. Caring tends to shrink your world — friends drift, social plans disappear, and the role slowly becomes your whole identity. Then there's the physical side pulling at mental health too: 67% of Irish carers have a diagnosed physical health condition, and 44% have experienced physical or emotional harm as a direct result of their caring role. One condition feeds the other. Yet 72% of carers have never accessed respite care — that one statistic says everything about how little space carers are given to breathe.

Support does exist, even if it's hard to find. The Carer's Support Grant — a €2,000 tax-free payment made automatically every June — won't solve everything, but it can open a door to a break, a few sessions of counselling, or just something that's yours. Carer's Allowance also allows you to work up to 18.5 hours per week while still qualifying, and from July 2026, the income disregard is increasing — to €1,000 per week for a single person — which means far more carers will be eligible. Knowing what you're entitled to is a small act of self-care in itself.
