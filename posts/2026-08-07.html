Home Care Coalition Launches 2026 Pre-Budget Submission Amid Irish Staffing Crisis

<cite index="44-1">The Home Care Coalition published their 2026 Pre-Budget Submission on August 5, as pressure mounts on home care services facing recruitment and retention challenges.</cite>

There are 624,000 family carers in Ireland, according to Family Carers Ireland — people who get up every morning and give everything to keep a loved one safe, comfortable, and at home. The State of Caring 2026 report found that 71% of those carers are struggling to make ends meet, while 48% experience severe loneliness. This week, Care Alliance Ireland published the Home Care Coalition's Pre-Budget 2027 submission, pushing the Government to do more for carers ahead of October's Budget. The message is clear: the system is improving, but there's still a long way to go.

The biggest recent win for carers is a change to the Carer's Allowance means test that came into effect on 2 July 2026. The Department of Social Protection increased the weekly income disregards — the amount you can earn before your payment is affected — from €625 to €1,000 for a single carer, and from €1,250 to €2,000 for a couple. A two-adult household with a joint income of up to €110,000 can still receive full Carer's Allowance. The payment itself is worth €270 per week if you're under 66 caring for one person, €308 if you're 66 or over, and up to €405 or €462 if you care for two or more people. If you were knocked back before because of the means test, it's worth checking again — you can call Citizens Information on 0761 07 4000 or apply online at mywelfare.ie.

On top of Carer's Allowance, there's the Carer's Support Grant — €2,000 paid every June, tax-free, no means test, and no strings on how you spend it. Over 147,000 carers received it on 4 June 2026. If you're already getting Carer's Allowance, Carer's Benefit, or Domiciliary Care Allowance, you get it automatically — no form needed. If you're not on any of those payments but you're providing full-time care, you can still apply using form CSG1, available from your local Intreo Centre or on gov.ie. The grant is paid per person you care for, so if you're looking after two people, you're entitled to €4,000.

If your loved one needs hands-on help at home — getting dressed, personal care, meals — the HSE Home Support Service can provide that free of charge. You don't need a medical card or an income assessment to qualify. You apply by filling in the HSE Home Support Service application form and returning it to your local Home Support Office, or you can ask your GP, public health nurse, or a social worker to help. After the HSE receives your form, they arrange a Care Needs Assessment to decide what support is needed. In June 2026, new legislation was passed to regulate home support providers through HIQA, meaning all approved providers must meet the same minimum standards — a real step forward for quality of care. You can find your nearest Home Support Office at hse.ie/homesupportservice.

If you're caring for someone right now and you're not sure what you're entitled to, ring Citizens Information on 0761 07 4000. They're free, friendly, and will give you a straightforward breakdown of exactly what payments and services you could be getting — including whether the new July 2026 income limits mean you now qualify for Carer's Allowance for the first time. You've been showing up for your loved one every day. This is one phone call that might start showing up for you.
