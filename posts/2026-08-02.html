State of Caring 2026 Reveals Severe Financial and Emotional Crisis for Irish Family Carers: 71% Struggle to Make Ends Meet

Family Carers Ireland's latest data shows over half of Irish family carers cut back on essentials like food and heating while providing over 90 hours of unpaid weekly care.

Somewhere in Ireland right now, a woman in her fifties is dropping her teenage daughter to school, rushing home to help her elderly mother get dressed, and then logging on to work — all before 9am. She is part of what researchers call the sandwich generation: people caught between caring for ageing parents and raising children of their own. People aged 50–59 are the single most likely group in Ireland to be providing regular unpaid care. With Ireland's 65+ population having grown 37% since 2015 and projected to double by 2051, this particular squeeze is only going to tighten.

Over 500,000 people in Ireland provide unpaid family care, according to Family Carers Ireland. The average carer puts in 38.7 hours a week — a full working week, without pay, on top of everything else. For sandwich carers, those hours sit alongside school runs, homework, teenage heartbreak, and their own jobs.

Most sandwich carers are doing it quietly, without any formal support in place. According to the State of Caring 2026 survey by Family Carers Ireland, 72% of Irish family carers have never accessed respite care. The toll is significant: 48% of family carers have been diagnosed with a mental health condition, and 67% have a physical health condition. Among those with physical health conditions, 40% carry a back injury directly linked to their caring role.

There is also a gender dimension. According to Family Carers Ireland, 34% of women in Ireland do most or all of the care for their main recipient, compared to 19% of men. For many women in the sandwich generation, this is not a choice they consciously made — it is something that accumulated, quietly, over years of being the one who shows up.
